## The premise

A single mountain — **the Hill** — sits in the middle of a contested borderland
nobody could hold for long. At its summit is a thing of ultimate power: a
relic, a throne, a network of ancient cap-points, an engine of old war — the
exact nature is TBD with the players in session zero. Whoever holds the summit
gets a **temporary boon** — supplies route to them, allies rally, intel flows.
**Permanent control** is the campaign end-state, and the climax decides it.

The KotH twist: control of the summit **shifts** through the campaign in
discrete "control rounds." Holding the Hill is never a one-time victory. The
party will lose the Hill. They will retake the Hill. They will lose it again.
The question is not "who holds it now" — it's "who holds it when the last
horn sounds."

## The factions (one per player, to be picked / refined in session zero)

Three seed factions. Players pick one each, may reskin freely, may co-author
the details. None of these are villains by default — each has reasons.

### Mann Co. Industrial

Bombastic, profit-driven, blast-happy. Wants to **extract and monetize** the
Hill — strip-mine the relic, bottle the power, sell what they don't blow up.
Headed by some inheritor of the old Saxton lineage. The "sells features and
gets in fights with regressions" energy. Comically over-equipped; tactically
chaotic.

- **Strength**: gear, gear, more gear, explosives.
- **Weakness**: every decision must justify itself to a quarterly board.
- **Internal voice**: "if it's worth a thing, it's worth a thousand things."

### The Order of the Standing Hour

A scholarly esoteric order. Wants to **study and preserve** the Hill — the
relic is a riddle, the Hill is a library, blowing it up is sacrilege. Robes,
candles, careful steps. Long memories. They've watched the Hill change hands
for centuries and have records of every prior attempt.

- **Strength**: knowledge — the Order knows things the others don't.
- **Weakness**: hesitation. Every "let me consult the archive" is a turn lost
  to faster factions.
- **Internal voice**: "what the Hill *is* matters more than what it *gives*."

### The Wind-Walker Clan

A nomadic warrior clan that has **claimed and defended** the Hill as ancestral
ground for generations — at least, that's the story. Travels light. Strikes
hard. Vanishes into the badlands when the odds turn. Honor-bound; oath-keepers;
death-before-dishonor types — but not stupid, and willing to ally.

- **Strength**: mobility, terrain knowledge, hit-and-fade tactics.
- **Weakness**: numbers. They cannot hold the Hill against a sustained siege.
- **Internal voice**: "the Hill is not for sale, not for study, not for any
  living hand to keep."

## Tone & genre

Action-comedy with real stakes. TF2 by way of pulp fantasy. Big personalities,
bigger weapons, somehow-emotional moments in the middle of absurd setpieces.
Players are heroes and idiots in equal measure; consequences land; victories
feel earned.

## Hard rules

- **No death without player consent.** A failed save means consequence
  (capture, injury, lost gear, social cost, relic damage) — not corpse.
- **2d6 + modifier** for major decisions (PbtA-ish). 10+ clean success. 7–9
  mixed success — you get what you want with a cost. 6– complication.
- **The Hill cannot be permanently controlled until the climax.** Until then,
  holding the summit is always temporary — measured in scenes, not in
  campaigns. Plan accordingly.
- **NPCs are named when introduced.** Once named, they stay named — log to the
  journal so they don't drift.
- **Continuity over completeness.** If improvising a detail contradicts
  established canon, the canon wins. Renegotiate at the table if needed.

## Starting situation

> *To be co-authored with the players in session zero. The opening scene
> below is a placeholder.*

A storm sits over the Hill. The cap point at the summit has just changed
hands — nobody at the bottom yet knows who holds it. Every faction's
scouts are watching. The party is somewhere on the flank — together, by
choice or coincidence — and the next twenty minutes will decide whether
they go up, go around, or go home. Choose your faction. Choose your scene.
Roll initiative when ready.
